# Aloha Capital (Aloha10x) — Brand Brief for Short-Form Video

## Positioning statement

> Aloha Capital finds the overlooked commercial property — the vacant strip
> center, the raw industrial shell, the underleased office — and turns it
> into an asset worth multiples of what we paid, through disciplined
> acquisition and development. Investors get access to that upside through
> our fund, without sourcing, underwriting, or developing a single deal
> themselves.

The wedge (confirmed by two rounds of trend research in
`trend-research.md`): the dominant format in real-estate social content is
the **before/after transformation** — and it's also the single best-
performing pattern found (a grain-silo-to-home reel hit 1.7M views/136x a
creator's median; a 35-acre land timelapse hit 1.5M/180x). Aloha's actual
business *is* a before/after transformation story, at commercial scale,
backed by real numbers instead of a DIY renovation. That's the creative
engine for this brand — every video should be able to point at a real
project's before-state, after-state, and the number in between.

## Audience — two tracks, per the PPM

Aloha10x's PPM offers two distinct products (see `knowledge-base.md`), so
content should speak to two audiences rather than one:

1. **Note/debt investors** — want predictable, fixed real-estate-secured
   income, smaller check size, capital-preservation-minded. This is the
   audience closest to what generic "passive real estate income" content
   on Reels/TikTok targets.
2. **Profit-participation investors ($500K+)** — want direct equity-style
   upside in specific deals, sophisticated, already comfortable with
   real estate risk, likely already doing (or has done) deals themselves
   and is evaluating whether to keep doing the work personally or back a
   sponsor who does it at scale. This is the audience watching "underwrite
   a multifamily deal in 60 seconds" and "3 ways to make money with
   landlocked land" — DIY commercial real estate education — who would
   rather have Aloha do it and participate at the project level.

Most short-form content should lead with the transformation story (appeals
to both), then branch the CTA/offer explanation by check size.

## Voice

- Confident and asset-specific, not hypey. Credibility comes from naming a
  real property, a real purchase price, a real exit — not adjectives.
- **Every total-return number gets its hold period attached, every time**
  ("400% over a projected 5-year hold," never a bare "400%"). This is both
  a compliance requirement (`knowledge-base.md`) and, done right, more
  persuasive than a bare percentage — specificity signals rigor.
- "Projected" stays attached to unrealized deals; only 3 Waterside Crossing
  gets to be described as an outcome, not a target.
- Educational undertone even when the hook is aspirational: briefly show
  *why* the asset was overlooked and *what* Aloha did (repositioned a
  vacant retail box into NNN-leased retail; converted raw industrial into
  cold storage; repositioned an office building) — the mechanism is the
  credibility, not just the multiple.

## Visual identity direction (for Premiere/Higgsfield/HeyGen)

- **Palette**: warm neutral base (sand, warm white) + one confident accent
  — deep ocean teal or a rich green. Avoid generic finance-bro black/gold.
- **Typography**: bold condensed sans for on-screen numbers and captions —
  the before/after trend research shows big, simple on-screen text
  (purchase price, exit price, "SOLD") outperforming subtler treatments.
- **Signature visual motif — the before/after split or reveal**: drone
  establishing shot of the property "as bought" → hard cut or wipe → the
  stabilized/developed result, with the dollar figures animating in over
  each state. This is the visual backbone across all three concepts below,
  matched directly to the highest-performing pattern found in research.
- **Document/proof inserts**: closing documents, executed purchase
  agreement, a sale HUD statement (redacted) for the realized deal —
  signals "this really happened," same function drone/property b-roll
  serves for the visual transformation.
- **Avatar (HeyGen)**: Abdel's avatar as the on-camera narrator/principal —
  talking-head + b-roll intercut is still the dominant surrounding format
  even when the centerpiece is a before/after reveal.

## Content pillars

1. **Transformation / brand explainer** — lead with a specific project's
   before/after, then explain the two ways to invest (fixed note vs.
   $500K+ profit participation) so both audiences self-select (Concept 1).
2. **Myth-vs-mechanism** — contrast the DIY flip/landlord content dominating
   the feed with Aloha's institutional-scale, fund-based version of the
   same idea (Concept 2).
3. **Proof/realized case study** — 3 Waterside Crossing, the one deal with
   an actual closed sale, walked through as a real outcome, not a
   projection (Concept 3).
4. *(Future)* Once more projects close or stabilize, rotate the
   transformation pillar across Franklin Produce, Baltimore Light Street,
   etc. — the format is reusable per-project.

## Compliance guardrail (repeat from `knowledge-base.md` — do not skip)

Every script with a specific project number is draft-for-review, not
publish-ready, until Aloha10x's compliance/legal contact confirms (a) the
figures are current and cleared for public use, (b) required disclosures,
and (c) whether the platform/audience needs accredited-investor gating.
"Projected" vs. "realized" and "total return" vs. "annual return" language
must be preserved exactly as drafted, not simplified away in editing.
