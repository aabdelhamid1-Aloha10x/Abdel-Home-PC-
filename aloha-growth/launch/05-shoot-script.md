## PART 1: THE TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Read each block as its own take. Bold words get a little extra weight. `(beat)` means pause one second.

### BLOCK 1: HOOK (about 25 sec)
If you own a daycare, and your phone rings at nine p.m. because a teacher called out, a parent is upset, or a bill is due, (beat) then you don't have a business yet. You have a very demanding job.

I'm going to show you what changes that. And I'm going to start with a **truck**.

### BLOCK 2: THE TRUCK (about 40 sec)
In 2020, I rented one truck. (beat) That was the entire company.

About fourteen years earlier, I'd moved from Egypt to the United States. I didn't come from money. I had a finance degree and a lot of ideas, and I started several businesses. Transportation became the biggest one.

That one rented truck became A and H Logistics.

### BLOCK 3: THE NUMBERS (about 55 sec)
Let me show you what happened.

In 2021, we did two hundred twenty thousand dollars. (beat)
In 2022, three hundred forty thousand. (beat)
In 2023, **three point four million.** (beat)
In 2024, eleven point five million. (beat)
And in 2025, **sixteen point seven million.**

Look at the jump between 2022 and 2023. I didn't suddenly work ten times harder. I couldn't have. What changed is that I stopped running everything myself, and I started building a company that could run **without me in every room.**

### BLOCK 4: WHAT I LEARNED (about 1 min 20 sec)
If you're building a business right now, the number isn't the part I want you to focus on. I want you to think about what has to change between running everything yourself and building a company that can grow.

Here are the three things I'd look at.

**First, understand your numbers.** Revenue is only part of the picture. What does it cost you to deliver? What's left after expenses? And when does the cash actually come in? More sales can create more pressure if the operation isn't ready for them.

**Second, make the work repeatable.** If every question comes back to you, start writing down the answers. Who owns the task? What does a good result look like? How do you check it? That gives your team something clear to work with.

**Third, find the real bottleneck before you spend more money.** Sometimes you need more leads. Sometimes you need better follow-up. Sometimes sales are fine, but delivery is falling behind. Those are different problems, and they need different solutions.

### BLOCK 5: WHERE THIS GETS STUCK IN A DAYCARE (about 55 sec)
[ADJUST TO YOUR OWN EXPERIENCE: keep only what you've really seen.]

In a daycare, these same problems show up in a few places.

Empty seats. Not because families aren't out there, but because an inquiry sits for hours, the tour doesn't turn into an enrollment, and nobody follows up.

A team that runs on the owner. Every decision comes back to you, because nothing is written down.

And numbers nobody has looked at closely: what it costs per child, what's left after payroll, when the cash actually arrives.

### BLOCK 6: WHY I'M TALKING TO DAYCARE OWNERS (about 50 sec)
Since then, I've taken the same approach into other businesses.

In 2023, I started Aloha Capital. It's real estate investing, and today we have over twenty-five million dollars in assets under management.

I'm also a partner in a daycare. I helped take it from zero to a six-figure business in less than a year.

And I built Aloha Growth, where we help business owners improve their operations, sales, and marketing. Different industries. Same question: are you building a business, or a job that needs you for everything?

### BLOCK 7: HOW WE WORK (about 1 min)
Here's how it works. Three steps.

**Step one, Diagnose.** We step inside your center. We look at your numbers: tuition per child, cost per child, payroll, margin, cash timing. We look at enrollment: how fast you respond, how many tours turn into enrollments. And we look at operations and people: your ratios, your scheduling, your turnover, and what only you can do.

**Step two, Blueprint.** You get a written plan that names your number one bottleneck and lays out the next twelve months, phase by phase, with owners and metrics.

**Step three, Build.** If you want, our team carries out that plan with you: the enrollment system, the tour and follow-up scripts, the procedures, the hiring and training, and the reporting. And for centers that are ready to grow, we help plan the next location, including the real estate side through Aloha Capital.

### BLOCK 8: THE FOUR WAYS TO START (about 40 sec)
There are four ways to start.

The **Fill Sprint.** Thirty days. We fix how you answer, tour, and follow up with families, and we launch one campaign to fill your open seats.

The **Growth Blueprint.** Three weeks. A full diagnostic and a twelve-month plan.

**Fill and Systemize.** Ninety days. We build the enrollment system, the procedures, and the retention system with you.

And **Scale Partner.** Twelve months, for centers ready to open or acquire their next location, with us as your operating partner.

Let me answer the questions I expect.

"I don't have time." That's exactly why the plan starts by taking work off your plate, not adding to it.

"I've hired marketing help before." Marketing only fixes one of the three problems. We look at the whole center first, so you're not spending on ads to send families into a process that loses them.

"Can I afford this?" That's why there's a Fill Sprint. It's a focused way to start. [CONFIRM: mention your pricing or payment options only if you've decided them.]

It's **not** for you if you're looking for a quick trick, or you're not willing to change how the center runs.

## PART 2: SHOOT PLAN

### Gear checklist
| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Camera | Your best phone on the rear camera, 4K at 30 fps, locked exposure and focus. Or a mirrorless camera. |
| Tripod | Stable, camera at eye level |
| Teleprompter | A teleprompter app or a prompter rig with the camera behind the glass. Set 150 words per minute. |
| Audio | A lavalier or shotgun microphone close to you. Audio matters more than video. Record in a quiet, soft room (curtains, rugs, carpet). |
| Lighting | Two soft lights: a key at 45 degrees from the camera, and a dimmer fill or hair light behind you. Turn off overhead lights and mixed colours. |
| Background | Tidy, warm, out of focus, about 6 feet behind you. Or the same blue studio background as your profile photo. |
| Backup | Second memory card, battery, and a phone charger |

### Wardrobe and look
- Navy polo (matches the profile photo and the brand). Avoid stripes, tiny patterns and bright white.
- Angle your glasses slightly down to avoid reflections, or use anti-glare lenses. Check a test shot.
- Groom the night before. Matte powder on the head if it shines.

### Framing and delivery
- Medium close-up, chest up. Eyes at the top third line. Look **into the lens**, not at the script.
- Shoot 16:9 in 4K. Leave headroom so we can also crop to 9:16 and 1:1 for ads.
- Speak as if you're talking to one daycare owner across a table. Calm, direct, warm. Not a stage voice.
- Energy map: Blocks 1-3 confident and warm. Block 4-5 slower, thoughtful. Block 6-8 clear and upbeat. Block 9-10 honest and direct. Block 12 warm and inviting.
- Smile on the truck and the daycare stories.

### How to record
1. **Slate every take:** say "Block 4, take 1" out loud, clap once, then wait three seconds before speaking.
2. Record **three takes per block**: one calm, one a little more energetic, one as a safety.
3. If you stumble, stop, breathe, and start the sentence again cleanly. Leave a pause. Don't cut yourself off mid-word.
4. After each block, record five seconds of silence for room tone.
5. Record **pickups** for every number: say each year and figure two extra times.
6. Record "reaction" clips: nod, smile, look down and up, small laugh. These help the edit.
7. Total on-camera time: about 60-75 minutes.

### Optional second angle
A side or slightly wider angle for cutaways. Use it for Blocks 4 and 7.

### B-roll to film (real footage beats stock)

**Children:** do not film identifiable children without signed parent and center releases. Prefer empty rooms or shots from behind.
